Question: A program has a name in capitals at the top, such as FORWARD. Why give a number a name?

You change the number in one place, and every line that uses the name changes with it. The name also says what the number is for.

Question: What does the line for count in range(4): do to the lines inside it?

It runs them 4 times, in order, one after another.

Question: The turn line counts degrees of motor turn. A quarter turn is 90 degrees of the robot's turn. Are those the same number?

No. Your team found its own number of degrees of motor turn for a quarter turn by testing.

Question: How did your team find its number for QUARTER_TURN?

By testing: change the one number, run, and write down what the robot did, until it faced along the second strip.

Chunk 1 of 3

The colour sensor measures reflected light

The colour sensor shines a light at the floor and measures how much of the light comes back. That measurement is called reflected light. It is a number from 0 to 100. More light coming back gives a bigger number.

Line 5 names the port of the colour sensor. Line 9 measures the reflected light once and prints the number in the console, the part of the SPIKE App that shows what a program prints.

from hub import port
import color_sensor
import runloop

LINE_SENSOR = port.B


async def main():
    print(color_sensor.reflection(LINE_SENSOR))


runloop.run(main())

Line 5 says port.B. If your colour sensor's cable goes to a different port, change the letter on line 5 to your port's letter.

Chunk 2 of 3

Move until the line

Choose a number between your two readings and put it on line 7. Its name is DARK. A reading less than DARK means the sensor is over the tape.

Line 15 starts the wheels, and they keep turning. It has no await, because it only starts the move. Line 16 waits until the reflected light is less than DARK. The sign < means "is less than". Line 17 stops the wheels.

from hub import port
import color_sensor
import motor_pair
import runloop

LINE_SENSOR = port.B
DARK = 50


async def main():
    # The left drive motor is in port A. The right drive motor is in port E.
    motor_pair.pair(motor_pair.PAIR_1, port.A, port.E)

    # Start the wheels. Wait until the sensor is over the line. Stop the wheels.
    motor_pair.move(motor_pair.PAIR_1, 0, velocity=250)
    await runloop.until(lambda: color_sensor.reflection(LINE_SENSOR) < DARK)
    motor_pair.stop(motor_pair.PAIR_1)


runloop.run(main())

What the page works out

Computed, not measured

DARK = 0: the test is never true, the wheels are never stopped
DARK = 101: the test is true at once, the wheels stop straight away

The first one: no. The robot moves over the far line and keeps moving, because no reading is less than 0. The test on line 16 is never true, so the hub never reaches line 17. Press the button in the middle of the hub.

The second one: it hardly moves at all. Every reading is less than 101, so the test on line 16 is true at once and line 17 stops the wheels straight away.

Put your own number back on line 7 before the mission.

Try DARK = 101 as well and watch what the robot does.

Hinge question · from today's chunks

Before the mission

    await runloop.until(lambda: color_sensor.reflection(LINE_SENSOR) < DARK)

Question: Suppose a team measured 80 over its floor and 10 over its tape. These two numbers are made up for this question. Which value of DARK makes the robot stop at the line: 5, 40, or 95?

40. It is between the two readings. Over the floor the reading is 80, which is not less than 40, so the hub keeps waiting. Over the tape the reading is 10, which is less than 40, so the hub goes on to the stop line.

With 5 the robot never stops, because 10 is not less than 5. With 95 it stops at once, because 80 is already less than 95.
