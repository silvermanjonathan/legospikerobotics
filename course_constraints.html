2. What the course never uses

  • Word Blocks. This course is written in Python throughout.
  • while True or any loop with no stop the student can name.
  • if inside if. One if may sit inside one while, as in the line follower.
  • Lists, and variables that change while the program runs.
  • A second task running at the same time.
  • Seconds as the unit of a move. Moves are counted in degrees of motor turn or ended by a sensor.
  • Any distance unit other than cm.
  • The medium motors and any part not on the port map.

What part 1 leaves for part 2

  • if, while, and functions the students define. They arrive in sessions 7 and 8.
  • The hub's speaker, light matrix, and motion sensor, and the second colour sensor. They arrive in sessions 5 to 7.
  • Any speed other than velocity=250. Part 2 adds 100 and 150, and lets teams tune the speed.

3. One way to do each thing

One frame, at the top and bottom of every program:

from hub import port
import motor_pair
import runloop


async def main():
    # The left drive motor is in port A. The right drive motor is in port E.
    motor_pair.pair(motor_pair.PAIR_1, port.A, port.E)

    # Move forward. The wheels turn 720 degrees of motor turn.
    await motor_pair.move_for_degrees(motor_pair.PAIR_1, 720, 0, velocity=250)


runloop.run(main())

One way to move until the sensor says stop:

motor_pair.move(motor_pair.PAIR_1, 0, velocity=250)
await runloop.until(lambda: color_sensor.reflection(LINE_SENSOR) < DARK)
motor_pair.stop(motor_pair.PAIR_1)

One start, one forward direction, one measuring point: behind the start line, front edge on the line, and every distance measured to the front edge.

4. Standing rules

  • One change per run.
  • Predict before you run.
  • The Expected column is filled in before the run.
  • The robot starts in the same place, facing forward, every run.
  • Names say what they do: QUARTER_TURN, not x.
  • The frame at the top and bottom of every program.

5. Locked hardware and software

  • The robot: LEGO's Advanced Driving Base, one for each team of four, built from the SPIKE Prime set and the SPIKE Prime Expansion Set.
  • The port map: port A left drive motor, port E right drive motor. See the robot page.
  • The wheel: one full turn moves the robot about 28 cm.
  • The field: see the field page.
  • The SPIKE App, version 3, and the hub's software, frozen at the versions on the reference robot. Write both version numbers here when the reference robot is set up.

LEGO adds no new features to the SPIKE App after 30 June 2026 and supports it until 30 June 2031 (LEGO Education).

6. Locked decisions

  • Part 1 is four sessions and stands on its own. Session 4 is its demo for families. Part 2 is four more, for a class that continues, and session 8 is a second demo.
  • Session 1 has no opener. That time goes to building the robot.
  • The final mission of part 1 is told to students in session 2.
  • Machinery handed over, then opened: the frame (async def main():, runloop.run(main())) is handed over in session 1 and explained in session 8. A class that stops after part 1 never sees it opened.
  • Machinery handed over and not opened: the word lambda, from session 3, and [0] after tilt_angles(), from session 6.
  • Deliberate errors: DARK = 0 in session 3, chunk 3, and two lines in the wrong order in session 4, chunk 2. First tries that every team replaces: 180 for the turn, 50 for DARK, port.B and port.F, 900 for QUARTER, and 30 and 150 in the line follower. Session 5, chunk 3, shows numbers that start at 0 on purpose.
  • Standards: New York science (engineering design) and New York mathematics only. No computer science codes: the graph returned none for New York on 12 November 2026.
