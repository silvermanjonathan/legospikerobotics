The challenge mat

Three lanes, three robots, four missions, 170 points.

The mat

The challenge mat is a field for three robots at once. It is made of 24 sheets of letter paper taped together, 4 sheets across and 6 sheets down. It is about 86 cm wide and 168 cm tall.

The mat has three lanes, one for each robot. Every lane has the same marks, so one program works in any lane. There are four missions. Each robot that does a mission scores points. When all three robots do it on the same run, the class scores a bonus. There are 170 points in all.

The challenge mat from above, drawn to scale. The dashed grey lines are the edges of the sheets. Each robot starts on the left of its lane and moves forward to the right.

Make the mat

  1. Print the file at 100 % size on letter paper, with the paper upright. In the print window choose Actual size, not Fit to page.
  2. Check one sheet with a ruler. The bar in its bottom left corner is 5 cm long.
  3. Page 1 is a picture of the finished mat. The other 24 pages are the sheets. Each sheet has its name in its bottom right corner: a letter for its column and a number for its row.
  4. Each team tapes one lane. Lane 1 is rows 1 and 2, lane 2 is rows 3 and 4, and lane 3 is rows 5 and 6. Lay the 8 sheets out face up, edge to edge, like the picture. Do not cut anything off and do not overlap the sheets. The white strip around each sheet is part of the mat.
  5. Turn the sheets over two at a time and tape every seam on the back. Tape on the front can catch a wheel.
  6. Join the three lanes, lane 1 at the top. Put the mat on a hard, flat floor and tape its corners down.

Three teams, one score

The three teams are not playing against each other. The class has one score, and the biggest points come only when all three robots do a mission on the same run.

  • A run starts when the teacher counts down. All three teams press the button on their hub at the same moment.
  • A team that has its robot working helps the next team: read their numbers, watch their run, and say what you saw.
  • Teams may share numbers such as QUARTER_TURN, but each team tests a shared number on its own robot before a scored run. Two robots built from the same book can still need different numbers.

The match

In the last session the class plays a match. A match is 3 minutes long, and all three robots are on the mat for all of it.

  • The class runs the missions in any order, one mission at a time.
  • Between missions each team may pick its robot up, put it back at a start, and choose another program on the hub.
  • Once a run starts, nobody touches a robot until all three have stopped.
  • A mission can be run again. Its best run counts.
  • One builder from each team keeps the score for another team's lane and reads its ruler.

Room for three robots

  • The robots' paths are 56 cm apart. In mission 2 a robot reaches about 38 cm below its start, and in mission 4 its front edge reaches the bottom edge of its own lane. Neither reaches the next robot, which sits about 7 cm inside its own lane.
  • Every robot turns to the right, so no two robots turn toward each other.
  • If the room cannot fit a mat 168 cm long, leave the lanes apart. Each lane is a complete field 86 cm by 56 cm.

How the mat differs from the session pages

  • Session 2's mission square has sides of about 55 cm. That square does not fit a lane. On the mat the square has sides of about 28 cm, which is FORWARD = 360.
  • Session 3 moves the far line between runs. The mat cannot move its line, so the robot starts from A or B instead. The far line is 55 cm from start A and 41 cm from start B.
  • Session 4's drop zone is a square of tape 40 cm on a side. A lane's drop zone is 27 cm by 22 cm.

Not yet checked on a robot

  • The mat was drawn from the wheel arithmetic: one full turn of the wheel moves the robot 27.63 cm. The places of the drop zone, the island, and the home marks also assume a robot about 22 cm long and 20 cm wide whose wheels are about 11 cm behind its front edge. Nobody has run a robot on this mat.
  • Print and tape one lane first. Run session 2's chunk 3 program and session 4's chunk 1 program on it from start A. If the robot touches a crate on the island, or its big wheels do not end inside the drop zone, change FORWARD and tell the class the new number.
  • Read the colour sensor over the white paper and over the printed far line, as session 3 does for the floor and the tape. Printed black can read higher than tape. If the two readings are close, lay black tape over the printed line.
  • Each sheet keeps a blank strip about 1 cm wide at its edges, because most printers cannot print there. No mark that a mission depends on crosses a seam, except the top and bottom of a drop zone, where a gap does no harm.
  • There are 23 cm of mat behind start A. A longer robot hangs over the mat's left edge at the start, which does no harm on a flat floor.
