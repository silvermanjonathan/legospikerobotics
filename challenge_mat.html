The challenge mat

Twelve sheets of paper, four missions, 80 points.

The mat

The challenge mat is a field for the class robot. It is made of 12 sheets of letter paper taped together, 4 sheets across and 3 sheets down. It is about 112 cm wide and 65 cm tall. It has four missions, and each mission scores points. There are 80 points in all.

The challenge mat from above, drawn to scale. The dashed grey lines are the edges of the 12 sheets. The robot starts on the left and moves forward to the right.

Download the mat to print (PDF, 13 pages)

Make the mat

  1. Print the file at 100 % size on letter paper, with the paper on its side. In the print window choose Actual size, not Fit to page.
  2. Check one sheet with a ruler. The bar in its bottom left corner is 5 cm long.
  3. Page 1 is a picture of the finished mat. The other 12 pages are the sheets. Each sheet has its name in its bottom right corner: a letter for its column and a number for its row.
  4. Lay the sheets out face up, edge to edge, like the picture. Do not cut anything off and do not overlap the sheets. The white strip around each sheet is part of the mat.
  5. Turn the sheets over two at a time and tape every seam on the back. Tape on the front can catch a wheel.
  6. Put the mat on a hard, flat floor or table and tape its corners down.

The match

In the last session each team plays a match. A match is 2 minutes long.

  • The team runs the missions in any order.
  • Between missions a builder may pick the robot up, put it back at a start, and choose another program on the hub.
  • Once a program starts, nobody touches the robot until it stops.
  • A mission can be tried again. Its best run counts.
  • A second team keeps the score on a sheet and reads the ruler.

How the mat differs from the session pages

  • Session 2's mission square has sides of about 55 cm. That square does not fit this mat. On the mat the square has sides of about 28 cm, which is FORWARD = 360.
  • Session 3 moves the far line between runs. The mat cannot move its line, so the robot starts from A or B instead. The far line is 55 cm from start A and 41 cm from start B.
  • Session 4's drop zone is a square of tape 40 cm on a side. The mat's drop zone is 21 cm by 38 cm, so that it fits inside two sheets.

Not yet checked on a robot

  • The mat was drawn from the wheel arithmetic: one full turn of the wheel moves the robot 27.63 cm. The places of the drop zone, the island, and the home marks also assume a robot about 22 cm long whose wheels are about 11 cm behind its front edge. Nobody has run a robot on this mat.
  • Before session 2, run session 4's chunk 1 program from start A on a taped mat. If the big wheels do not end inside the drop zone, change FORWARD in that program and tell the class the new number.
  • Read the colour sensor over the white paper and over the printed far line, as session 3 does for the floor and the tape. Printed black can read higher than tape. If the two readings are close, lay black tape over the printed line.
  • Each sheet keeps a blank strip about 1 cm wide at its edges, because most printers cannot print there. No mark that a mission depends on crosses a seam, except the long sides of the drop zone, where a gap does no harm.
  • There are 22 cm of mat behind start A. A longer robot hangs over the mat's left edge at the start, which does no harm on a flat floor.
