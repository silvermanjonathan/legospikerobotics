Opener · 10 minutes

What do you remember from session 1?

Talk each question through with your team before you open its answer. Your ledger card can help.

Question: Why does the robot start in the same place, facing the same way, on every run?

Then every run starts the same. If the robot ends somewhere new, the change came from the program, not from where the robot started.

Question: What does motor_pair.pair tell the hub?

It tells the hub which two motors turn the wheels, and which one is on the left and which one is on the right.

Question: A move line starts with await. What does await do?

It makes the hub finish that move before it starts the next line.

Question: When you test, why do you change only one number for each run?

Then you know which change made the difference. With two changes in one run you cannot tell which one did it.

Chunk 1 of 3

A name for a number

Line 5 gives the number 360 a name: FORWARD. Wherever the program says FORWARD, the hub uses 360.

A name in capitals at the top of the program is for a number you may want to change. You change it once, on line 5, and every line that uses the name changes with it.

from hub import port
import motor_pair
import runloop

FORWARD = 360


async def main():
    # The left drive motor is in port A. The right drive motor is in port E.
    motor_pair.pair(motor_pair.PAIR_1, port.A, port.E)

    await motor_pair.move_for_degrees(motor_pair.PAIR_1, FORWARD, 0, velocity=250)
    await motor_pair.move_for_degrees(motor_pair.PAIR_1, FORWARD, 0, velocity=250)


runloop.run(main())

Type the whole program into your new project. Line 5 starts at the left edge. It sits above line 8, so the name exists before any line uses it.

Predict: How many times does the robot move? About how many cm does it move in all, to the nearest 5?

Lines 12 and 13 both say FORWARD. What number does the hub use there?

360 degrees of motor turn is one full turn of the wheel, about 28 cm.

What the page works out

Computed, not measured

2 moves, each 1 full turn of the wheel
28 cm + 28 cm = 56 cm
to the nearest 5: 55 cm

The first one: 2 times. Line 12 is one move and line 13 is another, and await makes the hub finish line 12 before line 13 starts.

The second one: about 55 cm. Each move is 360 degrees of motor turn, which is about 28 cm, and 28 + 28 = 56. To the nearest 5, that is 55 cm.

Measure your own run from the start line to the robot's front edge. Then change line 5 to FORWARD = 180, say what you expect, and run it again.

Chunk 2 of 3

Find your quarter turn

A quarter turn is 90 degrees of the robot's turn. After a quarter turn to the right, the robot faces to the right of where it faced before, like the corner of a square.

The number in the program does not count that. It counts degrees of motor turn, and that is a different number. Line 6 gives it a name, QUARTER_TURN, and a first try of 180. Your team finds the real number by testing.

Chunk 3 of 3

A square, the long way and the short way

A square is four sides and four quarter turns. The long way is to write the forward line and the turn line four times: eight move lines. Open the program so far to see it.

The short way is the line for count in range(4):. It runs the lines inside it 4 times. The lines inside it start four more spaces in, so they are eight spaces from the left edge.

import motor_pair
import runloop

QUARTER_TURN = 180

What the page works out

Computed, not measured

forward, turn, forward, turn, forward, turn, forward, turn
4 quarter turns = 1 full turn of the robot
the robot ends where it started, facing the way it started

The first one: 4 forward moves and 4 turns. Lines 14 and 15 are inside line 13, so the hub runs that pair of lines 4 times.

The second one: back at the first corner, or close to it, facing the way it started. Four sides of the same length and four quarter turns close a square, and four quarter turns make one full turn.

Measure how far each wheel ends from its wheel marker. A small miss on each turn adds up over four turns, so this is a hard test of your number.
